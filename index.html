Project 1 · COMP3115

Does Weather Explain Air Quality?

Clustering US counties by air quality and climate · Group project, COMP3115 · Mar–Apr 2026 · My role: clustering analysis

The question

Los Angeles is famous for smog. Miami is hotter and more humid. If weather drove air quality, hot places should have worse air. I wanted to test whether counties with similar weather also have similar air quality.

The data

Air quality: EPA annual AQI summaries for 2025. I used three measures: Median AQI, 90th Percentile AQI, and the share of days rated "Good" (good ratio).

Weather: NOAA Local Climatological Data for 2025 from one airport station per state, summarised into mean temperature, mean humidity and total precipitation.

Counties: Six large counties from six states: Los Angeles (CA), Harris (TX), Queens (NY), Cook (IL), Miami-Dade (FL) and Denver (CO).

What I did

  • Cleaned and combined the two sources, filling missing weather values with the average of that measure.
  • Checked correlations first, to see whether weather and air quality moved together before clustering anything.
  • Explored each variable with histograms, boxplots and a temperature-vs-AQI scatter plot.
  • Scaled every feature to 0–1. Total rainfall ranges in the hundreds to over a thousand, while the other variables are far smaller, so without scaling rainfall would have dominated the clustering.
  • Ran K-Means with k = 3.

What I found

Air quality measures move together; weather barely moves with them. Median AQI and 90th Percentile AQI correlate at 0.96, and the good-day ratio moves in the opposite direction (−0.91 and −0.83). But mean temperature and Median AQI correlate at only 0.17.

The key insight: Miami-Dade and the Los Angeles/Harris group are all warm and humid, yet Miami-Dade has far cleaner air. The clustering separates them because of how many "Good" days they have, not because of temperature. Weather explains only part of the story. Traffic, industry, local geography and wildfire smoke probably explain the rest.

What I'd do differently

  • Six counties is a tiny sample, so the correlations are suggestive, not conclusive. Choosing k = 3 was also a practical choice (two counties per cluster on average), not a statistically tested one.
  • One airport station per state is a rough proxy for a county's climate.
  • Next time I'd use many more counties and add traffic and industry data to test the "human factors" idea directly.

Tools

Python, pandas, scikit-learn (MinMaxScaler, KMeans), matplotlib

Project 2 · COMP3115

Two Dimensions of Dirty Air

Reducing 14 air-quality indicators with PCA · Group project, COMP3115 · Mar–Apr 2026 · My role: PCA and clustering in PCA space

The question

County air-quality data has many columns that say similar things: days rated Good or Unhealthy, median and maximum AQI, and the number of days each pollutant was the main driver. Can those 14 columns be boiled down to a few dimensions that describe how US counties differ?

The data

EPA annual air-quality summaries for counties across the six states, with 14 indicators per county, including AQI category days, AQI maximum, 90th percentile and median, and days dominated by CO, NO₂, ozone, PM2.5 and PM10.

What I did

  • Standardised all 14 indicators (z-scores), so days and AQI points could be compared fairly.
  • Ran a full PCA to see how much information each component keeps.
  • Projected counties onto the first two components and plotted them.
  • Used the elbow method to choose four clusters, then ran K-Means in this reduced space.
  • Built a biplot to see which original indicators push each component.

What I found

One dimension carries about a third of the information. The first component alone explains roughly 36% of the variance, and the first two together about half. About five components are needed to reach 80%.

The two dimensions have clear meanings:

  • PC1 – general air quality: driven by the number of PM2.5 days, median AQI and unhealthy-air indicators. Counties on one side have generally cleaner air, counties on the other side have generally worse air.
  • PC2 – extreme events: driven by maximum AQI and upper-percentile values. A county can have a decent typical year and still suffer a few severe pollution days.

The key insight: Counties differ in two separate ways. Some are bad every day, others are mostly fine but hit by severe episodes. Looking only at average AQI would hide the second group.

What I'd do differently

  • Two components keep only about half the variance, so the 2D picture leaves out a lot. I'd check a third component too.
  • One extreme outlier county stretches the plot and is worth investigating separately (for example, a wildfire year) rather than leaving it in the picture unexplained.

Tools

Python, pandas, scikit-learn (StandardScaler, PCA, KMeans), matplotlib, seaborn

Project 3 · Internship

From Spreadsheet to Shop-Floor Report

Logistics and inventory reporting · IT internship, Suzhou Greenhulk Bio-Tech · Jul–Aug 2026 (270 hours)

The context

The company runs a factory that makes, packages and ships cleaning products and pharmaceuticals. Managers need to know, at a glance, how much raw material is on hand, how much product has been packed, and how many orders are waiting.

My role

As an IT intern working under senior colleagues, I recorded the logistics and inventory figures (raw materials, packaged products, order counts), and used them to produce basic visual reports so the team could monitor factory output more easily. I also learned the factory's internal software systems from the senior IT staff.

How the reports were built

  • Record: Log raw material quantities, packaged product volumes and order numbers.
  • Tidy: Keep the records consistent so they can be compared day to day.
  • Show: Turn the tables into simple charts that answer one question each.

What I learned

Good reporting starts with the reader's question, not the chart. IT support means understanding how people actually use the numbers every day, and working across departments matters as much as technical skill.

Tools

Spreadsheet and data-recording tools, factory management software, basic charting
